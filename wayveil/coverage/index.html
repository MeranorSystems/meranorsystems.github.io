Coverage & data transparency

Known data, honestly presented.

WayVeil can only reason about surveillance that is represented in its supported data. A route showing zero known ALPR encounters does not mean the road is surveillance-free.

The most important coverage rule

0 known ALPR means zero encounters in the current known/active supported dataset for that route. Unknown, newly installed, moved, inactive, incorrectly mapped, or otherwise missing devices can still exist.

Trust is a state, not a vibe.

Surveillance records should carry evidence quality instead of all being treated as equally certain.

VERIFIED

Supported by evidence strong enough to treat the device as known surveillance under the accepted data policy.

PROBABLE

Strong evidence suggests the device is relevant, but the record is not presented as fully verified.

UNVERIFIED

A lead or report that may be useful for awareness but must not silently become routing-known surveillance without the required trust promotion.

Cohort-One boundary

Missouri first — because supported beats broad.

Initial driving acceptance is intentionally limited to supported Missouri coverage. Expansion should happen only after the new region has its own data, provider, runtime, and safety evidence.
